## 8. Vehicle-Deployed UAVs and Support Vehicles

### 8.1 Deployable Support Vehicles

Some RVP vehicles can deploy support vehicles and switch control between the parent vehicle and the deployed vehicle.

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Reconnaissance UAV | SUAV light reconnaissance UAV, quadcopter reconnaissance UAV |
| Attack UAV | Quadcopter bombing UAV (drops PG-7V), quadcopter suicide UAV |
| Unmanned ground vehicle | RAWR unmanned combat vehicle |
| Radar relay vehicle | Buk-M3's 96L6 radar vehicle |

Parent vehicles: LAV-25, ZBL-08A, ZTZ100, and M142 can deploy the SUAV or quadcopter UAV; LAV-25, ZBL-08A, and ZTZ100 can also deploy RAWR; Buk-M3 deploys the 96L6 radar vehicle.

Gameplay example: an M142 launches a reconnaissance UAV, which marks enemy vehicles with its reconnaissance pod; the M142 then fires rockets directly at the enemy markers on the tactical map.

Deployment and control:

- Supports deployment, control switching, and automatic UAV circling.
- Support vehicles are limited by signal range and automatically recovered when out of range.
- Redeployment has a cooldown.
- While a support vehicle is deployed, the parent vehicle's seats are locked.

### 8.2 Buk-M3: Radar Vehicle and Launcher

Buk-M3 can deploy one 96L6 radar vehicle:

- After deployment, control can be switched and the two vehicles can be positioned separately.
- The radar vehicle provides target information and the launcher fires; the two vehicles are linked by datalink.
- The radar vehicle can serve as a radar relay.

### 8.3 Shipboard Helicopter Landing

- This is an experimental feature, recommended mainly for filming.
- Only the 052D currently has a helicopter landing pad.
- When a helicopter approaches the ship, press `P` to start automatic landing.
