# Real Vehicle Pack

A large add-on for **Limitless Vehicle** on Minecraft 1.20.1 / Forge. RVP adds a wide range of realistic weapons, ammunition types, support equipment, and gameplay mechanics on top of the LV vehicle framework. Its signature features include anti-radiation missiles, man-in-the-loop missiles, and extensive ammunition customization. The vehicles use detailed mesh models, hence the name **Real Vehicle Pack**.

Current version: `1.0.0_preview_0.6.2.1`. This is a preview release; content, values, and balance may still change. When reporting a problem, include the RVP version, base mod version, logs, and reproduction steps.

---

## 1. Scope

RVP adds the following on top of Limitless Vehicle:

| Module | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Weapon systems | Guns, rockets, missiles, aerial bombs, torpedoes, targeting pods, laser weapons, fire support, weapon customization |
| Support equipment | ERA, APS, active ECM, passive ECM, electro-optical jammer, DIRCM, flares/chaff/smoke, maintenance equipment |
| Modular damage | Damage resolved by part and module; independent durability, damage multipliers, failure effects, and repair order |
| Vehicle-deployed UAVs and support vehicles | UAVs, unmanned ground vehicles, radar relay vehicle, UCAV launcher; shipboard helicopter landing |
| Tactical map | GPS target designation, radar target list, artillery map, mini artillery map |
| Interface and controls | Man-in-the-loop HUD, HMD, focus lock, warnings, CCIP, equipment/module panel, action cards |
| AI crew | Gunner generator, behavior configuration, driving, weapon and radar operation, automatic countermeasures |

## 3. Installation

### 3.1 Before You Install

- RVP is distributed as a Forge mod. Place the `-full.jar` into `mods/`; do not extract it.
- Vehicle models, textures, sounds, and data are bundled inside the `-full.jar` and are extracted automatically on first launch.
- RVP requires Limitless Vehicle as a dependency.
- On first launch, RVP extracts its built-in vehicle pack to `limitless_vehicle/rvp/`.

### 3.2 Requirements

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Minecraft | 1.20.1 |
| Forge | 47.4.x (built with 47.4.13) |
| Limitless Vehicle | 0.6.2.1 |
| RVP | 1.0.0_preview_0.6.2.1 |
| Client and server | Both required; versions must match |

### 3.3 Steps

1. Install Minecraft 1.20.1 + Forge 47.4.x.
2. Place `ywzj_vehicle-1.20.1-0.6.2.1.jar` into `.minecraft/mods/`.
3. Place `ywzj_rvp-1.20.1-1.0.0_preview_0.6.2.1-full.jar` into the same `mods/` folder.
4. Launch the client or server once and wait for the built-in vehicle pack to be extracted to `limitless_vehicle/rvp/`.
5. Enter the game and confirm that RVP vehicles and items appear in the creative inventory.

## 5. Weapon Systems

The RVP data directory contains 231 weapon and ammunition definition files, including subordinate entries such as warheads and submunitions. RVP vehicles generally have multiple weapon stations for the main gun, secondary guns, machine guns, missiles, and rockets. Fourteen fixed-wing and rotary-wing vehicles support custom pylons; the F/A-18F and J-16 offer the widest selection.

### 5.1 Guns and Ammunition

RVP's guns include vehicle- and aircraft-mounted machine guns, autocannons and close-in weapon systems, tank guns, naval guns, and a vehicle-mounted laser suppression system. Different weapons use different ammunition; below is a summary of the main ammunition types by their actual in-game behavior.

- **APFSDS**: Fin-stabilized discarding-sabot armor-piercing round. High direct damage with no explosive payload; can penetrate 2 blocks, with direct damage falling off after passing through a wall. Effective against armored targets and highly resistant to explosive reactive armor.
- **HEAT**: Shaped-charge round. Higher direct damage plus a small explosion; the hit damage is reduced by ERA. Overall performance against both direct and explosive damage falls between APFSDS and HE. Extremely effective against lightly armored targets such as IFVs. Tank HEAT has no tandem warhead; tandem warheads are found only on missiles.
- **HE**: High-explosive round. Low direct damage, large blast damage and radius; relies on the explosion to kill soft and lightly armored targets.
- **PELE**: Penetrator with enhanced lateral effect. Lower direct damage, but releases a large number of fragments when hitting a vehicle or block; effective against light armor and infantry in enclosed spaces, limited against heavy armor.
- **AHEAD**: Programmable ammunition. Requires a radar or weapon-station lock to calculate lead and set the airburst fuze automatically. Without a lock it does not program an airburst and flies as a normal direct-fire round.
- **Programmable airburst**: Press the lock key (default `R`) to range the aim point (it must hit a block). The round detonates after traveling “measured range + offset” along its trajectory, useful for airbursts above cover or near a target.
- **SAP**: Wall-breaching high-explosive round with 1-block penetration; it can pass through a wall and detonate on a target behind it, mainly against infantry behind cover.
- **Shotgun**: Fires multiple pellets per shot, covering soft targets at close range; pellet damage falls off with distance.
- **Railgun**: ZTZ100 and AbramsX are equipped with an experimental railgun system.

### 5.2 Rockets

- **Unguided**: Hydra 70, Zuni, S-13, S-8KO, FS70B, 130-2 and other rocket pods. The ballistic computer provides continuous impact calculation.
- **Guided**: APKWS, FS70A and other laser-guided rockets. They follow the laser spot and can engage moving targets.
- **Cluster**: M30, M261, FS70B cluster types. They disperse many submunitions near the ground, with strong area effects against soft targets and limited anti-armor capability.
- **White phosphorus/incendiary**: M30 white-phosphorus airburst types. They scatter a white-phosphorus barrage near the ground for large-area incendiary effect.

### 5.3 Missiles

Missiles are grouped by guidance type.

| Guidance | Description and examples |
| --- | --- |
| Infrared (IR) | Seeker tracks the target's heat signature; AIM-9M, PL-10, FIM-92K, KD-88A, etc. HMD support enables high-off-boresight acquisition, lock, and launch |
| Active radar homing (ARH) | Midcourse datalink guidance, then the onboard radar seeker activates and acquires the target; AIM-120D, PL-15, AIM-54C, YJ-19, etc. |
| Semi-active radar homing (SARH) | Requires continuous illumination from the aircraft/vehicle radar; AIM-7P, R-27R, ESSM, etc. |
| Semi-active laser homing (SALH) | A laser designates the target; the illuminated target triggers a laser warning; AGM-114K, AGM-65E, LAHAT, AKD-10, etc. |
| Laser beam riding (LBR) | Flies along a laser beam; the illuminated target triggers a laser warning; 9M119M1, GP-125, 9M127, etc.; 9M119M1 and GP-125 are gun-launched missiles |
| Anti-radiation (ARM) | Homes on an emitting radar; shutting down the radar can evade it; AGM-88E, LD-10, YJ-91, etc. |
| Active infrared (AIR) | Radar or datalink midcourse correction, then an IR seeker searches autonomously in the terminal phase; HHQ-10, HQ-13, etc. |
| GPS/INS | Satellite and inertial navigation with tactical-map designation; AKF-98A, Storm Shadow, ATACMS, YJ-20, etc. GBU-31, LS-6, and GB-3 GPS are satellite-guided bombs, covered in 5.4 |
| Man-in-the-loop TV (HITL_TV) | After launch, the seeker video feed opens; the operator selects and locks a target in the feed, similar to War Thunder's man-in-the-loop missiles; KH-39, KD-88, etc. |
| Man-in-the-loop command-line TV (HITL_CLOS_TV) | After launch, the operator directly controls the missile's control surfaces to steer it, similar to Battlefield 4 TV missiles; TL-17, CM-502KG, Switchblade loitering munitions, etc. |
| SACLOS | The operator aims at the target and the missile follows the line of sight by command; harder to use than LBR but does not trigger a laser warning; TOW-2A/2B, HJ-73E, 95Ya6M, etc. TOW-2B uses an EFP top-attack warhead: it flies over the target and detonates downward, scattering fragments, effective against targets behind cover |

Some missiles have multi-mode seekers: for example, Brimstone uses a semi-active laser/active radar dual-mode seeker, flying toward the laser spot in the first phase and then activating its active radar seeker to acquire the target autonomously. Some missiles support dual-pulse motors, cold launch, high-loft/over-the-top trajectories, and proximity/impact fuzes.

### 5.4 Aerial Bombs

- **Conventional bombs**: MK-84 and similar unguided gravity bombs; the ballistic computer provides continuous impact calculation.
- **Semi-active laser-guided bombs**: GBU-24, GBU-48, GB-3 laser, GB-500, YJ-1000. Modified from conventional bombs; the carrying aircraft illuminates the laser spot and the bomb flies toward it, allowing engagement of moving targets.
- **Satellite/inertial-guided bombs**: GBU-31, LS-6, GB-3 GPS. GPS coordinates can be marked on the tactical map; after release the bomb flies toward the coordinates.
- **Infrared-guided bombs**: GB-3 IR. The IR seeker tracks target heat signatures and can engage moving targets.

### 5.5 Torpedoes

Torpedoes can travel 1–2 meters below the water surface and deal massive damage to large ships.

### 5.6 Pods

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Targeting pod | Marks targets; marked targets are displayed on the tactical map. |
| Coordinate designation pod | Obtains and marks target coordinates for the GPS panel or fire support; some models integrate “call fire strike / call airstrike” |

TGP is used by the EA-18G, F/A-18F, and J-16D; EOTS by the J-20A; the SUAV pod by the light reconnaissance UAV and quadcopter reconnaissance UAV. Coordinate designation pods with integrated fire support are used by the MBT co-pilot station, EA-18G, J-16D, and reconnaissance UAVs.

### 5.7 Laser Weapons

- ZTZ100's 10 kW laser suppression system can illuminate enemy vehicles; after enough hits it blinds players and AI gunners.
- While blinded, AI gunners can only use radar-guided or radar-midcourse weapons; players see a white screen that obstructs observation.
- Laser illumination triggers the corresponding warning and HUD indication.

### 5.8 Fire Support

- **RVP fire support terminal**: Hold it in the main or off hand and right-click, or press `K` to open the fire support map; supports call, impact area, round count, and ceasefire.
- **Fire support modes**: Hasty, effective, surveillance, and aerial strike.
- **Barrage patterns**: Point, linear, and creeping barrage.
- **Support ammunition**: Aerial bombs, rockets, cluster rockets, white phosphorus, ATACMS, 9M723, GPS cruise missiles, etc., depending on the fire support configuration.
- **Pod integration**: Targeting pods can mark entities or coordinates; vehicles carrying “call fire strike”/“call airstrike” can select a strike entry in the pod interface and automatically call it after marking coordinates.

### 5.9 Weapon Customization and Ammunition Selection

Most RVP vehicles support extensive weapon customization. Players can choose different ammunition or loadouts for the mission, for example:

- **SEAD**: aircraft carry anti-radiation missiles to suppress radar air-defense vehicles.
- **DEAD**: aircraft carry IR- or laser-guided missiles to strike air-defense systems with their radars off.
- **Ground vehicles**: use PELE against many lightly armored targets and APFSDS against heavy armor.

## 6. Support Equipment

RVP support equipment is configured by bone module and has independent durability and functional state. Destroyed equipment stops working and can be restored by repair.

| Equipment | Function |
| --- | --- |
| ERA | High damage-reduction multiplier; explodes and disappears after absorbing enough damage |
| APS | Detects and intercepts incoming ammunition by sector; if a sensor bone is destroyed, that sector stops intercepting |
| Active ECM | Jams radar/satellite/radio-guided missiles in range and becomes the strongest radiation source, attracting anti-radiation missiles. Powerful ECM on electronic-warfare aircraft can generate false signals that interfere with fighter radar and RWR |
| Passive ECM | Generates false targets when illuminated by enemy radar; stops when the bone is destroyed |
| Electro-optical jammer | Jams SACLOS missiles entering its sector, making them deviate |
| DIRCM | Directional infrared countermeasure against IR, AIR, man-in-the-loop missiles, and SBW's SA18; limited engagement channels can be saturated by multiple incoming IR missiles |
| Flares/chaff/smoke | Configured per vehicle with independent ammunition and cooldowns; flares decoy IR/AIR missiles, chaff decoys radar-guided missiles and breaks radar locks, smoke blocks laser spots and IR seekers |
| Maintenance equipment | Repairs parts and equipment |

The equipment panel shows support-equipment status and lets you set repair order. ERA and other support equipment use separate repair queues; when a bone has both ERA and another device, they recover together in the ERA queue.

## 7. Modular Damage

RVP resolves damage by part and module; different parts have independent durability, damage multipliers, and failure effects.

| Part/module | Failure effect |
| --- | --- |
| ERA | The hit block fails after being penetrated, loses its protection, and changes appearance |
| Engine | Severe damage greatly reduces power and halves top speed; complete destruction removes all power and mobility |
| Barrel | Severe damage may cause firing failures or a barrel burst; even successful shots lose accuracy. Complete destruction prevents firing. At stage-1 damage, missile-type weapons only succeed or fail; they do not cause a barrel burst |
| Traverse | Severe damage greatly reduces traverse speed; complete destruction prevents rotation |
| Fire control system (FCS) | Stabilizer fails, ballistic calculation and CCIP stop, thermal/night vision and EO search become unavailable; the crosshair remains but provides no valid ballistic solution |
| Radar | Destroyed radar cannot search or track targets |
| APS/jammer/DIRCM/countermeasure launcher | Destroyed equipment loses its function |
| Maintenance equipment | Destroyed maintenance equipment cannot perform quick repairs |

Damage calculation uses part OBBs and independent damage multipliers; explosion direction, impact angle, armor reduction parameters, distance, ammunition type, and ERA state all affect final damage.

The repair panel restores parts by priority; ERA and support-equipment repair orders can be set separately.

## 8. Vehicle-Deployed UAVs and Support Vehicles

### 8.1 Deployable Support Vehicles

Some RVP vehicles can deploy support vehicles and switch control between the parent vehicle and the deployed vehicle.

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Reconnaissance UAV | SUAV light reconnaissance UAV, quadcopter reconnaissance UAV |
| Attack UAV | Quadcopter bombing UAV (drops PG-7V), quadcopter suicide UAV |
| Unmanned ground vehicle | RAWR unmanned combat vehicle |
| Radar relay vehicle | Buk-M3's 96L6 radar vehicle |

Parent vehicles: LAV-25, ZBL-08A, ZTZ100, and M142 can deploy the SUAV or quadcopter UAV; LAV-25, ZBL-08A, and ZTZ100 can also deploy RAWR; Buk-M3 deploys the 96L6 radar vehicle.

Gameplay example: an M142 launches a reconnaissance UAV, which marks enemy vehicles with its reconnaissance pod; the M142 then fires rockets directly at the enemy markers on the tactical map.

Deployment and control:

- Supports deployment, control switching, and automatic UAV circling.
- Support vehicles are limited by signal range and automatically recovered when out of range.
- Redeployment has a cooldown.
- While a support vehicle is deployed, the parent vehicle's seats are locked.

### 8.2 Buk-M3: Radar Vehicle and Launcher

Buk-M3 can deploy one 96L6 radar vehicle:

- After deployment, control can be switched and the two vehicles can be positioned separately.
- The radar vehicle provides target information and the launcher fires; the two vehicles are linked by datalink.
- The radar vehicle can serve as a radar relay.

### 8.3 Shipboard Helicopter Landing

- This is an experimental feature, recommended mainly for filming.
- Only the 052D currently has a helicopter landing pad.
- When a helicopter approaches the ship, press `P` to start automatic landing.

## 9. Tactical Map

The tactical map shows terrain, players, vehicles, missiles, and GPS target points, and supports zooming, dragging, following, and quick marking. Right-click a target to bind it as a GPS target or fill its position into the GPS panel.

### 9.1 GPS-Guided Weapon Integration

- Select a target point on the tactical map and designate it as a GPS target.
- Supports single-point and multi-point marking.
- Multi-point mode manages multiple target points; radar mode selects targets from the radar panel.

### 9.2 Radar Target List Integration

- The tactical map can open the radar target list, showing distance, altitude, speed, NCTR identification, IFF, and lock state.
- Targets can be locked, and the current missile can be quick-fired if it meets the lock conditions.
- The radar target list can hide friendly targets.

### 9.3 Artillery Map and Mini Artillery Map

- **Artillery map**: after selecting a target, calculates elevation and impact distance with high/low ballistic solutions; left-click to fire; intended for RVP artillery weapons.
- **Mini artillery map**: shows a compact map in the screen corner when an artillery weapon is selected, computing the impact point from the current muzzle direction and elevation; left-click fires along the normal firing path. Accuracy is lower, so it is only for emergencies.

## 10. Interface and Controls

### 10.1 Man-in-the-Loop

- One of RVP's signature weapon features.
- Two interfaces: TV guidance (HITL_TV) and command-line TV guidance (HITL_CLOS_TV).

**HITL_TV**

- After launch, the missile's TV/thermal video feed opens.
- The operator selects a target point with the crosshair and presses the anchor key (default `R`) to anchor or re-anchor; the missile tracks that point.
- The HUD shows speed, altitude, distance, flight time, lock state, and operation hints.
- Supports color/black-and-white/thermal switching, mouse-wheel zoom, airburst detonation, and exit.

**HITL_CLOS_TV**

- After launch, the same TV/thermal video feed opens.
- The operator directly controls the missile's control surfaces; a steering cursor is shown at the screen center, with no target anchoring step.
- The HUD shows speed, altitude, distance, flight time, turn rate, and operation hints.
- Also supports video-mode switching, mouse-wheel zoom, airburst detonation, and exit.

### 10.2 HMD

- IR-guided missiles support HMD acquisition and off-boresight launch.
- Radar weapon stations support a radar HMD for quickly locking the direction being looked at.

### 10.3 Warnings and Indicators

- Laser illumination warning.
- Lock warning and missile-approach warning.
- Support equipment, DIRCM, ECM, APS, and countermeasure status.
- Hit markers, creature damage numbers, and module damage indicators.
- Action cards showing currently available actions and shortcuts.

## 11. AI Crew

- This is an experimental feature; it is recommended to use only friendly, hostile, and faction gunners.
- Supports seat selection and configuration-mode switching.
- AI crew can drive vehicles. Friendly gunners attack monsters and vehicles driven by hostile gunners; hostile gunners attack players, neutral creatures, and vehicles driven by friendly gunners; faction gunners attack players and gunners not in the same faction.

## 13. Configuration and Performance

- Client rendering and effects: `config/ywzj_rvp-client.toml`
  - Sight sensitivity, LOD scale, thermobaric particle quality/volume/flash/shake, explosion screen shake, volumetric trails, maximum distant vehicle renders, Distant Horizons compatibility mode.
- Client visual toggles: `config/ywzj_rvp-lighting-client.toml`
  - Screen-space lighting, explosion/muzzle/engine lighting, bloom, missile halo, EO jammer red glow, plume and air scattering.
- Common (client and server): `config/ywzj_rvp-common.toml`
  - Sight-axis acceleration, wreck lifetime, beyond-visual-range vehicle sync/authorization/distance, chunk leases and lookahead.
- HITL remote observation budget: `config/ywzj_rvp-hitl-remote.toml`
  - Session count, camera chunk radius, maximum control distance, chunk tickets/sends per tick, pending chunks, and extra entity limits.
- Server gameplay parameters: `config/ywzj_rvp-server.toml`

Performance recommendations:

- Allocate 6–8 GB of memory to the client.
- First launch extracts the vehicle pack from the ~234 MB jar; slower disks increase startup time.
- If performance drops, reduce thermobaric particle quality, screen shake, and distant vehicle renders; volumetric trails are off by default.
- When using Distant Horizons, select compatibility mode in the client configuration.
